## Visual Description
The lotus (Nymphaea caerulea or Nymphaea lotus) is depicted as a stylized overhead or three-quarter bloom with symmetrical petals radiating from a central seed pod. In profile, it shows a tight triangular bud tapering to a long straight stem. Colors: blue-white petals (N. caerulea) or pure white (N. lotus) with yellow stamens, green stem and base leaves. The flower head is always rendered with graphic clarity — bold outlines, flat color fills, and no cast shadows. The seed pod at center is rendered as a rounded or domed form with stippled dots indicating seed pockets.

## Canonical Constraints
- Petals are symmetrical and simplified — no naturalistic irregularity
- Stem is always perfectly straight and vertical
- Leaves (if shown) are flat, paddle-shaped, floating at water level
- Never shown wilting or partially open mid-bloom unless as a bud form
- Petal count is typically 8, 12, or 16 — always an even, symmetrical number
- Outline weight is uniform; no taper or calligraphic variation on petal edges

## Variants
- **Open bloom (overhead view)**: Used as field fill, column capitals
- **Profile bud**: Used in borders, offering scenes, held in hand
- **Lotus-and-papyrus composite**: Intertwined to symbolize Upper/Lower Egypt unity
- **Lotus emerging from primordial water**: Creation/solar rebirth context
- **Lotus throne**: Divine figure seated on lotus pad
- **Lotus scepter**: Single stem held upright by deity or noble as symbol of rejuvenation

## Composition Roles
- Column capitals in architectural registers
- Held in hand in offering scenes
- Border repeating element (alternating bud and bloom)
- Throne base for solar deities
- Cosmetic vessel and jar stopper form
- Field fill pattern in ceiling and floor registers of tomb paintings

## Safe Remix Knobs
- Color shift: violet or golden lotus remain iconographically consistent
- Can be simplified to pure geometric abstraction (4-petal cross) for borders
- Underwater root network visible on papyrus manuscripts
- Scale variation: micro lotus as jewelry inlay vs. macro lotus as architectural capital

## Avoid / Anti-Features
- Avoid naturalistic shading or volume
- Avoid asymmetric petal counts
- Do not render as realistic botanical illustration
- Avoid drooping or wilting stems — the Egyptian lotus is always idealized and upright
- Do not mix lotus with non-Egyptian floral forms (roses, lilies) in the same register
